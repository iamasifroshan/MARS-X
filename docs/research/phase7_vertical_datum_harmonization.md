# MARS-X — Phase 7 Research: Vertical Datum Harmonization

## 1. Principles of Mars Vertical Reference Systems

Planetary geodesy on Mars lacks a physical sea-level geoid. Instead, the authoritative vertical reference surface is the **MOLA Areoid**, defined by the Goddard Mars Potential Model (GMM-3) gravitational potential field, evaluated to spherical harmonic degree and order 50, where the mean equatorial radius is $3,396,190\text{ meters}$.

---

## 2. Prohibition of Arbitrary Numerical Shifts

A fundamental scientific principle enforced in MARS-X is:
> **No elevation product may be shifted or adjusted by arbitrary vertical offsets (e.g. guessing $\pm 1\text{ to }5\text{ meters}$) without documented geodetic evidence and transformation run logs.**

Stereo DTMs generated by photogrammetric packages (such as ISIS, SOCET SET, or Ames Stereo Pipeline) may contain localized vertical tilts or planar offsets resulting from spacecraft ephemeris uncertainties, camera jitter, or ground-point disparity mismatches. These offsets are spatially variable and non-uniform.

---

## 3. NASA Ames Stereo Pipeline (ASP) `pc_align` Geodesy

The ingested regional CTX DTM (`P21_009323_1754_XN_04S293W...`) was produced following the USGS Astrogeology controlled ARD workflow:
1. Stereo disparity correlation between CTX left/right panchromatic images (`P21_009323_1754` and `N02_062992_1754`).
2. Point cloud generation in Mars 2000 coordinates.
3. Rigid 3D transformation adjustment via ASP `pc_align` against MOLA Precision Experiment Data Record (PEDR) laser shot profiles.
4. Gridding to a 20.01 m/px Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF tied directly to the MOLA Areoid.

Because the CTX product has already been rigidly tied to MOLA tracks using documented ICP adjustment, MARS-X compares elevations directly on the MOLA Areoid while registering localized variance ($\sigma = 75.45\text{ m}$) as legitimate microtopographic detail rather than datum error.
